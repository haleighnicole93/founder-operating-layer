# Decision Log

A decision log prevents executive decisions from disappearing into meetings, messages, or memory.

The purpose is not to document everything. It is to capture decisions that create downstream work, dependencies, or commitments.

## Sample Decision Log

| Time | Decision | Context | Founder Input | Next Action | Owner | Due |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8:20 AM | Product pricing | 3:00 PM launch blocked pending pricing | Choose Option A or B | Confirm pricing with Product and unblock launch | Executive Partner | 10:00 AM |
| 8:20 AM | Candidate interview | Only availability conflicts with protected strategy time | Approve 2:30 PM interview | Book candidate and protect remaining focus time | Executive Partner | 9:00 AM |
| Pending | Customer escalation | Major customer requested founder involvement | Only if assessment confirms founder is necessary | Gather account context and recommend attendance or delegation | Executive Partner + Account Owner | 11:00 AM |

---

## After the Decision

Capturing the decision is only the beginning.

### Product Pricing

If the founder selects Option B:

1. Confirm the decision with Product.
2. Verify the launch is unblocked.
3. Update any stakeholders waiting on pricing.
4. Track the 3:00 PM launch.
5. Escalate only if a new blocker appears.

### Candidate Interview

If the founder approves 2:30 PM:

1. Confirm with the candidate and recruiting.
2. Update the calendar.
3. Preserve 2:00–2:30 PM and 3:00–4:00 PM as focus time.
4. Make sure interview context is ready beforehand.
5. Capture any hiring decision or follow-up afterward.

### Customer Escalation

If assessment shows founder involvement is necessary:

1. Confirm attendance.
2. Prepare a short briefing with issue, history, risk, and desired outcome.
3. Clarify who is leading the call.
4. Capture decisions and commitments.
5. Assign follow-up owners and deadlines.

If founder involvement is not necessary, the account owner handles the call and the founder receives only meaningful outcome or risk.

---

## Decision Log Principle

> A decision is not complete when the founder answers the question. It is complete when the resulting work has an owner, a deadline, and a path to closure.
